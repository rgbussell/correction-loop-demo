correction-loop-demo — build report

A continual-learning loop that learns from correction deltas — and can be seen refusing a bad round. Open data (VerSe 2020, CC BY-SA 4.0), MIT code. Updated 2026-09-13 · W0–W4: 5 rounds + 2 arms, tracked 28/28 tests passing

What this demo is

Production segmentation models are corrected by humans every day, and those corrections are usually thrown away. This demo builds the loop that keeps them: each round, a model meets a batch of new cases, its outputs are corrected, the correction delta becomes the training signal, and a retrained candidate is promoted only if it beats explicit null controls. The demo's job is to show the loop shape — including the round where the loop says no.

Three design commitments, made before any model exists:

  • Sequestration is a refusal, not a filter. Held-out test cases raising an error on contact with curation — never silently dropped.
  • Every promotion beats two nulls. A matched random cohort at equal N, and do-nothing. Improvement that can't beat "just add any cases" proves nothing about the selection rule.
  • One batch is poisoned on purpose (wrong-level enumeration). A loop that cannot be seen refusing a bad batch is theater.

What was done. scripts/build_partition.py scans every case directory, picks one CT series per case by a fixed preference order (CT-iso → bare → CT-ax → CT-sag), and derives each case's vertebral coverage from the reference labelmap itself (np.unique), not from metadata sidecars. Two honest findings along the way, kept in: (1) VerSe file naming is heterogeneous — verse* and GL* ids, dash and underscore series tags, and a bare-name series for a third of cases; the scanner handles all of them and skips 12 directories with no complete image+mask pair. (2) Centroid JSONs exist for only 26 of 119 directories in this mirror, which is why coverage comes from the masks. Every case's chosen series, coverage, shape and spacing is recorded in manifests/cases.json — the committed, diffable inventory the whole demo builds on.

Step 3 — The poison, declared and audited

Audit trail. manifests/poison.json records, per poisoned case: the source mask's sha256, the poisoned copy's sha256, the full label map applied, and the foreground voxel count (identical before/after — the poison relabels, never redraws). Originals are never touched; poisoned copies live outside git in data/poisoned/ and are substituted only when batch 2 "arrives". 13 cases, 189 labels shifted in total.

Step 4 — The ruler, null-tested and validated on the real poison (W1)

What was built. src/clloop/delta.py — the frozen correction-delta ruler. Per (auto, final) pair it reports three things: added path length (APL, mm — the contour a human would have to redraw, label-aware so identity errors carry their true cost; Vaassen et al. 2020), surface Dice (Nikolov et al. 2018 — deliberately label-blind, so its disagreement with APL becomes a diagnostic), and a per-level taxonomy: boundary / relabel / missing / spurious.

Why two scores that can disagree? Because their disagreement is the signature of the most dangerous failure class. An enumeration error leaves shapes perfect (surface Dice ≈ 1) while every renamed contour still needs redrawing (APL large). A single overlap score cannot see this; the pair cannot miss it.

Step 5 — The loop turns: baseline + round engine (W2)

What was built. A one-command round engine (scripts/run_round.py --round K): the incumbent model predicts the arriving batch, the frozen W1 ruler scores every correction delta on the native grid, the cohort is curated (in W2 still an accept_all stub that says so in its own record), the model retrains with a declared ~25% rehearsal mix, and the candidate is evaluated on the 24-case sequestered test set — per-region Dice for what it knows, ruler APL for what it would cost to fix. Every round writes a complete round.json: training mix, per-case deltas, evaluation, timing.

Scale honesty: a small MONAI 3-D U-Net at 3 mm isotropic, 2000 iterations/round — minutes per round on one consumer GPU. The demo measures loop mechanics, not segmentation SOTA; the coarse grid is a declared choice, and the ruler still scores on the native grid.

Step 6 — The run: five rounds, one poison, three verdicts (W4)

What a "round" is

A round is one turn of the deployment loop. Round 0 trains the baseline model on the initial pool only — the "deployed" model, strong on the anatomy it has seen (thoraco-lumbar) and blind to what it has not (cervical). Each later round simulates the passage of production time: a batch of new cases arrives, the incumbent model predicts them, the corrections those predictions would need are scored by the frozen ruler, and the loop decides — admit the batch and retrain (with rehearsal of older cases), or refuse it. The batches are deliberately ordered so the world changes: the cervical-containing fraction rises 14% → 38% → 62% → 77%, and the batch arriving at round 3 is the deliberately poisoned one. Ablation arms (the counterfactual, the no-rehearsal run) are side experiments that never enter the promotion chain.

The story the loop wrote, unscripted. The FIRST live poisoned round produced this demo's best exhibit: the admission screen's original rule (≥50% relabel case verdicts) MISSED the poison — the weak incumbent's boundary noise diluted 9/13 poisoned cases to "mixed", and the batch was admitted on evidence of 37/39 relabel levels agreeing on +1. The promotion gate then caught it: the poison-trained candidate scored −40.2% against the do-nothing null and was refused; the incumbent stood. The screen was revised to read the level-wise fingerprint (pinned by a regression test built from the real round record), and in the definitive run below it refuses the poison before any training. Layered nulls are the design, and the miss-then-catch is why.

The refusal, verbatim from the round record: "enumeration-shift signature: 39 relabel-class levels across the batch and 95% of their offsets agree on +1 — a systematic reference labeling error, refused before any training" — 39 relabel levels, consensus 95%, while the case-fraction that fooled v1 sat at 15%. A third honest detail: the forgetting gate ALONE would have passed the poisoned candidate (regional Dice drops ~0.02, within tolerance) — the poison corrupts enumeration, which region-Dice barely sees but APL fully prices. Three layers, three different failure classes.

Models are DVC-tracked (pointers committed). Per-round pushes failed at run time on an expired storage token — recorded in each round record rather than silently skipped — and were reconciled by a bulk push after re-auth (manifests/dvc_push_reconciliation.json; 7 pointers, 6 unique blobs: the refused round stored the incumbent byte-identically and content-addressing deduplicated it). Rounds 0–2 reproduced byte-identical losses across two independent chain executions (0.7627 / 0.6286 / 0.1172) — the seeded determinism G2 requires.

Was the test of this learning method successful?

Yes — as a test of the loop method, this run succeeded on every claim it was built to test.

  • Correction burden fell. Total sequestered-test APL: 2,730 → 2,044 m across the deployment — a 25.1% reduction — and every promoted step individually beat the do-nothing null (+10.0%, +9.0%, +8.5%).
  • New anatomy learned without losing the old. Cervical Dice 0.03 → 0.54 as the shifted batches arrived; thoraco-lumbar held (0.69/0.68 → 0.73/0.74) under the 25% rehearsal mix.
  • The loop refused a bad batch, and the refusal was worth it. The poisoned round trained nothing; the counterfactual arm priced the avoided damage at +40.2% burden. The layered design was exercised for real: screen v1 missed, the promotion gate caught, the screen was fixed with a regression test.
  • Reproducible and provenanced. Byte-identical training losses across two independent executions of rounds 0–2; an MLflow curve and a version-tracked model for every round.

What this does NOT show — the honest limits. A single seed, so the per-round gains carry no uncertainty band yet (phase 2's first job). The "corrector" is the reference mask, so the training signal is cleaner than real human corrections and is variance-reduction only. The model is deliberately small on a coarse grid, so absolute values say nothing about any clinical system. And one gentle round without rehearsal did not induce forgetting — the rehearsal margin (5.5% APL) is measured, but the catastrophic case is cited from the literature, not reproduced here. Within those bounds: the method works — the loop learns from correction deltas, defends itself, and proves both with artifacts.
